# Apple

Studied for one thing above all: **the product as a frame around the user's
own content**, which is exactly the Studio's job.

## What makes this product feel premium?

**Decisions already made.** The interface does not present its own complexity
as a set of choices; someone took responsibility for the defaults, and the
result is that a first-time user succeeds without configuring anything.

Premium reads as *certainty*. Nothing wobbles, nothing is approximate, nothing
is negotiable-looking. The confidence is in what is absent — there is no
settings drawer hedging the design.

## Layout principles

**Content first, chrome last.** In Photos, Music, Notes, the user's material
is the largest, brightest, most central thing; controls recede until wanted.
The interface is willing to look empty to keep the content dominant.

Generous, consistent margins. Alignment is exact — nothing is off by a pixel,
and that exactness is a large part of why it reads as expensive.

## Information hierarchy

One clear subject per screen, and a strong, unambiguous entry point into it.
Secondary capability is **reached, not displayed** — a second tap, a context
menu, a detail pane. The novice sees a simple product; the expert finds depth
where they expect it.

## Interaction patterns

**Direct manipulation.** You act on the thing itself rather than on a form
describing the thing. Where that is impossible, the control sits as close to
its effect as the layout permits.

Reversibility over confirmation. Undo carries far more weight than dialogs
asking "are you sure?", because a product that lets you recover does not need
to make you hesitate.

## Motion principles

Motion explains **spatial relationship**: a detail view grows out of the thumbnail
you tapped, so you know where you are and how to get back. Nothing decorative,
nothing that delays you, and everything interruptible — you can act during an
animation, never wait through it.

## Typography usage

One family, a disciplined scale, hierarchy from weight and size. Very few
levels. The result is that a heading actually reads as a heading, because
there are not nine competing weights teaching the eye to ignore them.

## Colour usage

Largely achromatic surfaces so the user's content supplies the colour. Accent
colour is scarce and therefore meaningful — it marks action and selection, not
mood. Semantic colour is unmistakable when it appears because it appears
rarely.

## What should Designakum learn?

1. **Be the frame.** The Studio's chrome must recede so the portfolio reads as
   the client's work, not as our template.
2. **Ship defaults, not options.** Take responsibility for the choice; that is
   what the client is paying for.
3. **Exactness reads as quality** more reliably than any decorative treatment.
4. **Depth is reached, not displayed.** Advanced controls exist; they do not
   greet you.
5. **Reversibility beats confirmation.** Especially in the editor.
6. **Achromatic surfaces let the user's work supply the colour.**

## What should Designakum NOT copy?

- **Its scale of restraint applied to onboarding.** Apple can assume a familiar
  idiom; our client is meeting a portfolio product for the first time and needs
  the first-run explained in words.
- **Discoverability by gesture.** Hidden affordances work when a billion people
  already know them. Ours must be visible.
- **Its typography as a system to import.** SF is theirs; our display face is
  an Arabic-first pairing, and the mark is an Arabic wordmark.
- **Marketing-page theatre** — full-bleed parallax and scroll choreography
  belong to a product page, not to a tool someone works in.
- **Assuming LTR.** Their layouts are designed LTR-first; ours cannot be.

# Framer

Studied with the most caution of the four: it is closest to what Designakum
sells and closest to what Designakum has decided **not** to be. The value here
is as much in the boundary as in the lessons.

## What makes this product feel premium?

**The output is beautiful before the user has done anything.** You begin from
something that already looks designed, and every subsequent action improves it
rather than assembling it. The emotional difference between editing something
good and building something from nothing is the whole product.

Its own interface is also a demonstration of its claim — a site-building tool
whose site is exquisite is arguing by example.

## Layout principles

A canvas dominates; controls live in a panel that is quiet, tight and secondary.
The user's work is always the largest thing on screen.

Preview and edit are the same surface rather than two modes to toggle between,
so the user never loses the sense of what they are making.

## Information hierarchy

Controls are grouped by **what the user is trying to change**, not by the
underlying property model. Only the properties relevant to the current
selection are shown — context does the filtering, so the panel never presents
the full universe of options.

Templates and starting points are given real hierarchy. The product understands
that choosing a direction is the hardest step and treats it as a first-class
screen rather than a menu item.

## Interaction patterns

**Choose a direction, then refine.** The flow is selection-then-adjustment, not
construction. Changes are visible immediately, which makes exploration cheap
and reduces the fear of touching anything.

Publishing is a distinct, confident, well-marked moment — not a save button
wearing a different label.

## Motion principles

Motion is used to teach: previews of an interaction show what it will do before
you commit. Because motion is the product's subject matter, its own interface
is deliberately restrained — the tool does not compete with the work.

## Typography usage

Editorial confidence in the marketing and template surfaces — large, generous,
sure of itself. Inside the tool, small and neutral so the canvas leads. The two
registers are kept strictly apart, and that separation is the lesson.

## Colour usage

Near-monochrome tool chrome so that the user's colours are the only meaningful
colour on screen. Accent is functional: selection, action, active state.

## What should Designakum learn?

1. **Never start from nothing.** The client should arrive at a draft that
   already looks good — this is the core of the Studio direction.
2. **Choosing a direction is a real step** and deserves a real screen, with a
   small number of strong options.
3. **Live preview, not a preview mode.** Editing and seeing are one surface.
4. **Group controls by intent**, and show only what applies to the current
   selection.
5. **Publishing is a moment**, designed and marked as one.
6. **Monochrome chrome so the client's work carries the colour.**

## What should Designakum NOT copy?

- **The canvas itself.** Free-form layout, drag-and-drop positioning, a layers
  tree, breakpoint editing, custom CSS — all explicitly ruled out. Layout is
  our job; a client who can produce a bad layout will.
- **Its depth of control.** Framer sells power to designers. We sell a finished
  presence to people who do not want to design a website.
- **The page tree.** There is one portfolio; sections, not pages.
- **Plugins and escape hatches.** Each one is a support burden and a way to
  break the presentation we are selling.
- **Its aesthetic**, which is a designer-tool aesthetic. Our register is the
  client's own professional credibility, not our cleverness.

> The distinction to hold on to: Framer makes **building** feel good.
> Designakum must make **not having to build** feel good.
